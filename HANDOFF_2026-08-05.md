## The one idea that explains almost everything

**Lightspeed Produce writes a PACK COUNT and calls it millilitres.**

`"1 ml"` of Yoghurt Greek Style [1Kg] means one 1kg tub. `"3 ml"` of black beans means
three tins. `"4 ml"` of pizza flour means four bags.

It hid for months because **the cost was always right** — it comes from the pack. So
totals looked sane while recipes read as gibberish and the builder quoted `$7350.00/L`
for yoghurt. Every symptom Zak reported this session was this wearing a different hat:
the $60.56/kg batch, the −574% GP, the $29,159/L pizza box, the 216g yield.

The detector: **divide what a line COSTS by our per-gram rate — that is what it really
uses.** If that disagrees with the stated quantity by 5x or more, the quantity is a pack
count. This is the sharpest tool in the repo; reach for it first.

## What to do next, in order

### 1. The 340 stale ingredients — one root cause, biggest prize
`build_costs.py` skips 589 invoice lines because `resolve_pack` reads only the
DESCRIPTION, and a liquor description is just `"BOMBAY DRY GIN"` with no size in it.
**$1,583 of gin invoiced since June never reached the cost book**; 13 cocktails price
off a January seed. Rooster Rojo ($3,230), Veuve ($969), Whispering Angel ($621) are in
the same state. `audit_book.py` ranks all 33 items over $100 by spend.

**The naive fix is wrong and there is a comment in `build_costs.py` saying so.** 1,152 of
the rejected lines carry an explicit `pack_qty`/`pack_unit`, but ILG records that as the
CASE (4.2L = 6x700ML) while pricing SOME lines per BOTTLE. I tried it: Patron Silver went
$2.94 → $0.61 a pour ($20 a bottle). Bombay's $296.60 really is a case; Patron's $76.52 is
one bottle; the same division is right for one and wrong by six for the other.

**The discriminator that does work:** test BOTH readings against the product's existing
seed rate and take the one that agrees.
- Bombay: 296.60 ÷ 4200mL = $0.0706/mL — seed says $0.070633 ✓
- Patron: 76.52 ÷ 4200mL = $0.0182 — seed says $0.0979 ✗, so it is a bottle

Write the test FIRST and make it assert **Patron Silver does not move**. That is the
regression that matters.

## Ground rules learned the hard way this session

- **Under-costing is the flattering direction and therefore the dangerous one.** A wrong
  number that looks plausible is worse than a stale one that is flagged. Two changes were
  reverted on this basis — read the "NOT DONE" comments in `build_costs.py` and
  `convert_lightspeed_recipes.py` before re-deriving them.
- **Do not round derived quantities.** Zak pushed back on this and he was right: rounding
  42.96 → 43 makes a guess look like a measurement. An ugly number is honest signal that
  nobody has weighed that ingredient yet. Sheet-derived numbers are exact; everything else
  should stay visibly derived.
- **The unit in Produce is not trustworthy — only the magnitude is.** Reading `"ml"` lines
  against per-gram prices took Rosemary Salted Fries from $1.86 to $0.0019, because
  Produce also writes `"0.35 ml"` for 0.35 KILOGRAMS of chips.
- **A miss that returns 0 is indistinguishable from a real answer.** I read `weeks` when
  the feed says `weekly`; every lookup returned 0 and it agreed with what we expected.
  `tests/test_sales_feed_schema.py` guards that now.
- **Verify UI edits three ways before shipping:** dangling `getElementById` check,
  `node --check` on the extracted script block, then `build_site.py`. This session that
  caught a `YIELD_QTY` reference that does not exist and a `getElementById('name')` when
  the field is `dish` — either would have frozen the builder.
