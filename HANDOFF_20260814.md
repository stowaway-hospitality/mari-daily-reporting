# Start here — cost book, 2026-08-14

Flags **85 → 81** after rebasing onto the day's upstream work. The number understates the work in one direction and overstates
it in another, and both are worth a paragraph.

## The finding that matters most

`_adjudicated_ids()` in `scripts/build_cost_book_flags.py` read **every row of
`data/product_map.csv` as a verdict on a price conflict.** It is a bridge table.

So running `python3 scripts/bridge_stale_seeds.py --apply` — 27 rows, every one a
mechanical name match — **silently retired the Frank's Hot Sauce and Honey Pure
conflicts.** Those two have a test comment that says, in as many words:

> The conflict is Lightspeed's to fix; our side is the evidenced one, and it
> should stay visible until it is fixed there.

Bridging OUR side to an invoice does not correct Lightspeed's recipe costing, so
it cannot answer "which of these two prices is real". The queue was being muted
by a maintenance script, and nobody would have noticed because the flags simply
stopped appearing.

Fixed: a product_map row now counts as a verdict only where the `confidence`
column records a **judgement** — the Havana Club pattern, `"pricebook-adjudicated;
ILG book price MAR 2026 ... so the $58.01 line is ONE bottle"` — and not a
**match**, `"name-prefix; seed $0.0084 -> invoice $0.0042/g (-50.0%)"`. Four
conflicts came back. **That is why 85 → 79 and not 85 → 75.**

## New file: `data/adjudicated_prices.yaml`

Verdicts with no price to import. Bridges go in `product_map.csv`; verdicts go
here; a verdict retires a flag without moving a number.

This file exists because ten of them were first written **into** `product_map.csv`
with blank supplier codes, and three tests went red inside a minute on
`purchasable_id()`'s ValueError — *"There is no natural key, so there is no
identity. Do NOT fall back to the description."* The refusal was right and the
rows were in the wrong file. `load_bridge()` would have skipped them safely; two
other consumers iterate the same file and do not.

12 verdicts. The good one is **Massenez Elderflower**: Back Office's `DefaultSize`
reads **50000 ml** on a cask the name calls `[5L]`, so Lightspeed's $0.004833/ml
is $253 ÷ 50 L of liqueur that does not exist, and our $0.0506 is right. That is a
*cause*, not a judgement — which is the standard the file asks for.

### A new detector, cheap and worth keeping

Sweep Back Office for a `DefaultSize` that is an exact power of ten off the size
written in the product's own **name**. Three exist; all three are now pinned
(Massenez 5L, Gyoza 800g, Mango-Chilli Puree 1L — the last is inert at $0.00 cost
and pinned only as a landmine). One of the three was a live 10x under-cost.

## Backed out — read this before re-deriving it

**Herb Chives `[Market]` = 6 bunches.** 17 paired invoices, median **exactly
6.00x**, eight consecutive same-day pairs to the cent. It is irresistible and it
is wrong, twice:

1. `data/pack_overrides.yaml` line 185 already carries **Zak's 2026-07-26
   confirmation** that FFT market herbs are sold *by the bunch*. A market bunch is
   one bunch about six times the size — which is exactly why it costs 6x. A ratio
   is not evidence of a pack count when a size difference explains it equally
   well, and the owner's answer about what arrives in the van outranks arithmetic.
2. The override landed on the **wrong observation** anyway. `costs.csv` showed
   only `bo-ingredient-seed` at $9.9000/can, so dividing by 6 looked safe — but
   22995343 also carries a `recipe-bridge-seed` at $1.65/ea, and after a rebuild
   *that* one wins the as-of lookup. It divided the bunch rate: **$0.275 chives**,
   with a new flag quoting $0.0027 lines to prove it.

**Reading `costs.csv` is not the same as knowing which observation WINS.** That is
the trap; it is now written into `pack_overrides.yaml` where the next person will
re-derive the fix.

## Deliberately NOT done

* **Broccolini `0.22 ml` and Guacamole `1 ml`** look like textbook
  `recipe_line_unit_fixes.yaml` candidates. They are not. The book **already**
  reads 0.22 as bunches and 1 as a tray, so the cost is right and only the Produce
  *label* is wrong. A `cost_factor` there would break two correct numbers.
* **Avocado stays per-tray.** A tray is 20 fruit ≈ 5000 g, but that 5000 g is
  *gross* — costing flesh grams against it under-costs by the peel-and-stone
  yield, and that yield is a kitchen weighing.
* **Lemon stays as it is.** One lemon is 200 g by BO's own DefaultSize, so the
  per-kg feed and the per-each record are the same fruit. But the seed's `ml`
  label means the 5 recipe lines on it are counts of fruit (0.08 = a wedge), and
  re-basing to grams would read 0.08 as 0.08 g — the Rosemary Salted Fries trap.
  ~$0.90 of recipe cost rides on it. Residual recorded, not buried.

## Two traps sprung today (in addition to the two in the 08-09 handoff)

* **`scripts/mutation_test.py` rewrites the working tree for ~25 minutes** and a
  file is only correct again *after* its own mutation finishes. A `git commit -A`
  run mid-flight **committed a live mutation** — `build_cogs_list.py`'s `DERIVED`
  tuple shipped as `("cost_per_unit_incl_gst",)`, precisely the defect the
  mutation exists to prove we catch. Caught only because a later `git status`
  showed the working tree disagreeing with HEAD *in the direction of correctness*.
  There is now a lockfile + dirty-tree refusal + a banner. **Never commit from a
  tree while it runs.**
* **A test can pass because of a file that is ABSENT from your laptop.**
  `test_no_token_is_unknown_not_a_false_all_clear` had been green for months.
  `health_monitor._workflow_failures()` falls back to the PAT on disk, so
  *repairing the PAT* turned "no token" into "ok" and the branch the test is named
  after had never run. Now isolates `HOME` and `ROOT` so tokenlessness is a fact
  rather than a coincidence.

## The mutation test found three holes — all now closed

`scripts/mutation_test.py` went **15/15 → 12/15**, and none of the three
survivors was a broken guard. Two of them were **the book getting clean**:

    check_pack_agreement --strict  ->  ok, no line sits on a whole pack factor (4201 rows)
    check_pack_as_rate   --strict  ->  ok, no seeded rate is its own container's price (4201 rows)

With nothing left to find, "tolerance too tight to ever match" returns the same
empty list as a working guard. **A detector with a clean corpus is
indistinguishable from a dead one**, so both now have FIXTURES — a planted 6x
case-as-bottle, a planted carton-of-40-as-one-piece, and a negative case each so
the fixture cannot pass with a guard that reports everything.

The third, "the invoice UOM stops outranking the description", survived the
*entire* gate. Nothing asserted the rule directly; what had covered it was live
data, and every test that pinned the affected ingredient was legitimately
rewritten as the data moved on. The rule was left naked.
`test_resolve_pack_uom_rank.py` now asserts the behaviour — including the two
refusals (a `6x700ML` multi and a `CTN-6` bulk label) that stop the rule becoming
the case/bottle bug somewhere new.

All three verified caught by applying the mutation directly.

## Six tests rewritten to guard the fix, not the defect

Every one of them was **pinning something it should have computed**: a membership
list, a count (a fourth Antica invoice arrived), a tray price ($26.40 → $22.00 is
a market moving, not a defect), two constants that each contradicted the
arithmetic written beside them, and 125 unauditable map rows where the stable
quantity is the **share** — 133/190 and 160/230 are both 70%.

One caution learned the hard way: loosening an assertion to survive real data can
open a hole. Relaxing the avocado check to a *range* let a mutation through
(`build_ingredients` no longer letting the invoice UOM outrank the description).
It is now **exact but derived** — it must equal the latest FFT `AH20T` invoice
read from the book itself, so a market move is not a failure while a misread pack
still is. Prefer *computed and exact* over *pinned* or *loose*.
